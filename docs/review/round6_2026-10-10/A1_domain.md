## MANDATORY FOCUS ITEMS

### Item 1 — Biological/clinical plausibility of the "day-7 bone cancer pain = type-I interferon + myeloid infiltration" claim

**【Problem】** The central mechanistic label "day 7 is an interferon event, not an ion-channel event" (§3.3, manuscript line 113) overstates the evidence and contradicts the paper's own neuronal data, while the myeloid-inflammation backbone is largely sound.

**【Evidence】** §3.3 (line 115): interferon-stimulated control module mean moderated t = +24.9, q = 6.8×10⁻⁵; glial/immune +11.4; individual Cd68 +2.86, Aif1 +2.18, Gfap +1.33, Ccl2 +0.81, Il1b +0.80. Table 3 (lines 289–297): at the *marker* level Cd68 bone-D7 +2.84 and Aif1 +2.19 are indeed strongly up, and Scn10a (Nav1.8) is *down* (−0.46) in bone cancer. So ion-channel/neuronal genes are not "absent" — they are down-regulated, and the paper itself attributes that to compositional dilution (§3.3 line 117). The title "not an ion-channel event" is therefore a rhetorical overstatement: Nav1.8 down-regulation is a well-established bone-cancer-pain (BCP) DRG finding (Miao et al., BMC Cancer 2010, Nav1.8 bilateral down-regulation in Walker-256 DRG at days 16–19), and the discovery dataset Zhai 2021 (ref [10]) titled its own paper "ion channels and cytokines," which the manuscript cites only as a data source and never engages as a competing interpretation. External literature check: a STING/TBK1/NF-κB activation paper in a rat BCP model reports IL-1β/IL-6/TNF-α and upstream STING in L4–5 DRG neurons — STING is a canonical type-I-IFN inducer, so an interferon programme in BCP DRG is biologically *plausible* and even mechanistically supported. Myeloid/glial activation in BCP is also well supported, but the supporting literature is overwhelmingly *spinal cord* (GFAP/OX-42 microgliosis, TLR4; "Neuroinflammation involving astrocyte and microglia … Walker 256," spinal cord), not DRG. Applying "glial/myeloid activation" to DRG is defensible (DRG has resident macrophages, not microglia), but the paper should not imply its DRG finding recapitulates the spinal-cord literature.

**【Why it matters】** The paper's single strongest conclusion ("type-I interferon event") is the hook of the whole biological narrative and the pivot of the disagreement with ref [14]. If the label is softened to "a prominent interferon-stimulated-gene programme with myeloid marker up-regulation, superimposed on a compositional dilution of neuronal transcripts," the conclusion survives and is more defensible. The current absolute phrasing invites a reviewer in the IFN/pain field to challenge whether 8 hand-picked ISGs (see Item 3 / _limma.py CONTROL_MODULES) constitute proof of a *type-I* (vs type-II or cytokine-driven) interferon response, and whether day-7 specifically (not day 14) is the pain state.

**【Specific fix】** Replace the §3.3 heading and first sentence. Paste-ready:
> "**§3.3 Day 7 shows a prominent interferon-stimulated-gene programme and myeloid marker up-regulation, on a background of neuronal-transcript dilution.** At day 7 the interferon-stimulated-gene control module carried the largest mean moderated t in the study (+24.9, q = 6.8×10⁻⁵), followed by glial/immune (+11.4) and Toll-like-receptor (+6.4) modules; individual myeloid markers Cd68 (+2.86) and Aif1 (+2.18) rose sharply. Neuronal modules — including voltage-gated Na⁺ and the Nav1.8-encoding Scn10a — were down-regulated, an effect the compositional controls (pan-neuronal, erythrocyte) place within a shift in cellular transcript share rather than selective ion-channelremodelling. Whether the ISG signature reflects a type-I interferon programme specifically (versus a broader cytokine-driven innate-immune response) is probed in §3.8."

### Item 2 — Is the disagreement with ref [14] (Baruch 2025) fairly and accurately represented?

**【Problem】** The attribution to ref [14] is factually accurate, but the statement that the conflict "our design cannot adjudicate" (§3.8, line 159) understates the paper's own evidence, which actually *contradicts* the neuronal part of Baruch's claim; and the paper does not note that Baruch's neuronal IFN is itself an injury/regeneration programme congruent with the paper's own "neuronal-injury-driven" reading.

**【Evidence】** I verified ref [14] (Baruch EN et al., *Nature* 2025;646:462–473, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09370-8). Its abstract states: "The injured neurons respond by autonomously initiating IL-6- and type I interferon-mediated inflammation to promote nerve healing and regeneration," and mechanistic validation includes neuronal *Atf3* conditional knockout and *Ifnar1* knockout. This is a cancer-immunology study of perineural invasion (cSCC, melanoma, gastric cancer) and anti-PD-1 resistance; B16F10 is its melanoma model, and GSE292522 is the DRG single-cell dataset it generated. So the paper's claim that ref [14] "reports a neuron-intrinsic type-I-interferon response in this model" (line 159) is **correct, not a strawman.** However: (a) Baruch's IFN-I is explicitly downstream of ATF3 and framed as a *nerve-healing/regeneration* programme — i.e., it is an injury response, exactly the "neuronal-injury-driven" programme the manuscript says B16F10 shows (Sprr1a +1.55, Atf3 +0.84, §3.8 line 155). The two studies therefore agree on the presence of a neuronal injury programme and differ mainly on whether ISGs are transcriptionally up-regulated in neurons. (b) The manuscript's own per-compartment test found ISGs *down* in neurons (Ifit2 in neurons q = 0.046, §3.8 line 157; Table 5 line 329 "Ifit2↓ neurons"). That is direct evidence *against* a neuronal-intrinsic IFN up-regulation at its resolution — so the conflict is partly adjudicated, not "cannot adjudicate." (c) The legitimate non-adjudicable part is the myeloid compartment, correctly noted (CD45 depletion, 46 myeloid cells total). The paper conflates the two.

**【Why it matters】** Credibility risk. A careful reader who knows Baruch 2025 will see that (i) the "disagreement" is narrower than framed, (ii) the paper actually has contradictory neuronal-compartment data it dismisses, and (iii) the paper omits that Baruch's IFN is an injury programme — which would have *strengthened* the paper's "model-specific neuronal-injury" thesis rather than weakened it. Honest framing here protects the paper from a Baruch-coauthor reviewer.

**【Specific fix】** Replace the sentence at lines 159–161 ("We also note a genuine discrepancy … a conflict our design cannot adjudicate … divergence is between models as much as between aetiologies"). Paste-ready:
> "We note a genuine, narrower discrepancy with ref [14]: that study reports a neuron-intrinsic type-I-interferon (IFN-I) response in injured neurons of the same B16F10 model, whereas at pseudobulk resolution with n = 4 + 4 animals we find interferon-stimulated genes flat or down in every testable compartment, including the neuronal bin (Ifit2 down, q = 0.046). Two caveats bound the comparison. First, ref [14] describes the neuronal IFN-I signal as downstream of ATF3 and part of a nerve-healing/regeneration programme — i.e., an injury response consistent with the Sprr1a/Atf3 neuronal-injury signature we observe; the studies therefore agree on a neuronal-injury programme and differ on whether ISGs are transcriptionally up-regulated in neurons at our resolution. Second, our per-compartment test does bear on the neuronal claim (and reads negative), but pseudobulk averaging across all neurons limits sensitivity to a sub-neuronal population, and the myeloid compartment — where ref [14]'s programme could also reside — is untestable because the source study depleted CD45⁺ cells. The robust statement is that the Walker-256 *interferon* programme is model-specific; the B16F10 model instead shows the neuronal-injury programme shared across nerve-injury aetiologies."

### Item 3 — Marker choice validity (Cd68/Aif1, Gfap, galanin)

**【Problem】** Cd68/Aif1 are appropriate DRG myeloid markers and galanin's injury-association is correctly interpreted, but Gfap is a weak DRG glial marker and the paper's own companion dataset demonstrates this — yet §4 still lists "glial activation (Gfap up)" among its *secure* conclusions.

**【Evidence】** Cd68 (macrosialin) and Aif1 (IBA1) are standard macrophage/monocyte markers expressed by DRG-resident macrophage-like cells and infiltrating monocytes; their up-regulation (Cd68 +2.84 bone-D7, Aif1 +2.19, Table 3 lines 289–290) is a valid readout of myeloid infiltration. Galanin is correctly called "injury-associated": it rises in paclitaxel (+1.92, Table 3 line 291) where axonal degeneration dominates, and galanin is a classic axotomy/nerve-injury-induced neuropeptide; the B16F10 neuron bin shows galanin +1.47 (§3.8 line 330), internally consistent. The problem is Gfap. The paper's own §2.8/§3.8 caveat states the satellite-glia bin "carries almost no Gfap transcript" — 7 unique-molecule counts across all eight animals, zero in five (line 97; line 159: "7 unique-molecule counts across all eight animals, zero in five of them"). In DRG, satellite glia express low GFAP (GFAP is a stronger marker of reactive CNS astrocytes and non-myelinating Schwann cells), so bulk Gfap +1.33 (§3.3) / +1.50 (Table 3) is a real but *ambiguous* signal. It is also the *weakest* of the strongly-up genes in the glial/immune module (Cd68 +2.86, Aif1 +2.18, Gfap +1.33), so leaning on it for "glial activation" overweights the least informative member.

**【Why it matters】** §4 (line 167) asserts as "secure": "Bone cancer pain … is an innate-immune event: type-I interferon signalling, myeloid infiltration, glial activation (Gfap up)." The Gfap-based "glial activation" leg is the one component the paper's own data undermine. A domain reviewer (or a glial-biology reviewer) will flag that GFAP is a poor DRG glial marker, and the paper's internal caveat already concedes this for the mouse dataset — the inconsistency between §2.8/§3.8 and §4 must be resolved in one direction.

**【Specific fix】** In §4 (line 167), drop the parenthetical "(Gfap up)" from the secure list and add a reconciling clause. Paste-ready:
> "Bone cancer pain at its transcriptional peak is an innate-immune event: type-I interferon signalling and myeloid marker up-regulation (Cd68, Aif1), with the neuronal compartment diluted rather than specifically remodelled. We note Gfap was mildly up in the rat bulk DRG (log₂ +1.33) but interpret this cautiously: in the companion mouse dataset the satellite-glia compartment carried almost no Gfap transcript, consistent with GFAP being a weak marker of DRG glia, so we do not claim glial activation as established."

### Item 4 — Clinical pharmacology claim in §4 is overstated for the evidence

**【Problem】** The sentence "chemotherapy-induced neuropathic pain responds partly to agents acting on neuronal excitability, whereas bone pain carries an inflammatory component that responds to anti-resorptive and anti-inflammatory strategies [1,2,16]" (§4, line 167) is not supported by either the n=4 rat DRG data or by refs [1], [2], [16] as cited.

**【Evidence】** The data presented are rat bulk DRG, n=4 per group (§2.1; §5 line 179). The inferential leap from "Cd68/Aif1 up in rat DRG" to "bone pain carries an inflammatory component that responds to anti-resorptive and anti-inflammatory strategies" is unsupported: bisphosphonates/denosumab (anti-resorptive) treat bone-metastases pain by inhibiting osteoclasts, not by targeting the DRG myeloid/IFN programme the paper describes; and refs [1] (ESMO cancer-pain guidelines) and [2] (NCCN adult cancer pain) are general analgesia ladders that do *not* stratify therapy by DRG immune vs neuronal mechanism. Ref [16] (Luger et al. 2005 Bone Cancer Pain: from model to mechanism to therapy) emphasizes osteoclast activation, NGF, endothelin-1 and nerve sprouting — not IFN/anti-inflammatory DRG targeting. For CIPN, the evidence-based agent is duloxetine (an SNRI, serotonergic-noradrenergic), which is not primarily an "agent acting on neuronal excitability"; gabapentin/pregabalin (α2δ ligands) have weak/negative CIPN evidence. So both halves of the sentence are loose, and the citations do not bear the mechanistic weight placed on them.

**【Why it matters】** This is the only place the paper makes a translational/clinical claim, and it is precisely where the n=4 rat, single-platform, no-human-validation evidence is thinnest. An editor or clinician reviewer will read this as the paper over-claiming clinical relevance, which damages the credibility of an otherwise careful methodology paper. It also contradicts §5 (line 181) which correctly says "nothing here is validated in humans."

**【Specific fix】** Replace the clinical sentence in §4 (line 167). Paste-ready:
> "The split is consonant with, but does not by itself establish, the clinical distinction that bone-metastases pain is partially refractory to opioids and responsive to anti-resorptive agents (bisphosphonates, denosumab) whereas CIPN is managed with agents such as duloxetine [1,2]; our data suggest a mechanistic correlate — a DRG myeloid/innate-immune programme in the bone-cancer model versus a neuronal-injury programme in the chemotherapy model — that future human or ex-vivo DRG work should test directly. We caution that this translation is hypothesis-generating: both datasets are rat, n = 4 per group, and no human validation exists (§5)."

### Item 5 — Must-cite literature gaps

**【Problem】** The biological discussion omits directly relevant mechanistic literature that would support (and refine) its interferon claim, and it cites the discovery dataset [10] only as a data source while contradicting its conclusion without acknowledgement.

**【Evidence】** (a) **STING pathway in BCP:** a published rat Walker-256 BCP study reports STING/TBK1/NF-κB activation in L4–5 DRG neurons with elevated IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α — STING is a canonical type-I-IFN inducer, so this is the most direct *mechanistic* support for the paper's interferon finding and should be cited alongside [3], [4]. (b) **The discovery paper's own interpretation:** Zhai 2021 (ref [10]) titled its work "ion channels and cytokines" and the manuscript's §3.3 headline ("interferon event, not an ion-channel event") directly contradicts it; the paper must engage this rather than cite [10] only for provenance. (c) **DRG myeloid/macrophage-in-pain literature:** the bulk of "glial/myeloid activation in BCP" evidence is spinal-cord microgliosis (GFAP/OX-42, TLR4); the paper should cite the DRG-resident-macrophage literature to justify using Cd68/Aif1 as DRG myeloid markers. (d) **CIPN DRG injury programme:** Atf3/Sprr1a/galanin as nerve-injury markers in paclitaxel/oxaliplatin DRG are established; the paper should cite foundational CIPN DRG transcriptomic work beyond the discovery set [11]. (e) The 2025 *Nature Methods* benchmark [9] is correctly cited — good.

**【Why it matters】** Without (a) and (b) the interferon claim looks like a novel over-reach rather than a finding placed in (and reconciled with) the existing BCP mechanistic literature; without (c)–(d) the marker choices look asserted rather than grounded. These are easy, high-value citations that a domain editor will expect.

**【Specific fix】** In §1/§3.3 and References, add: (i) the STING/TBK1/NF-κB BCP-DRG study; (ii) an explicit sentence in §3.3 acknowledging Zhai 2021's "ion channels and cytokines" conclusion and stating why the reanalysis elevates the ISG programme; (iii) one DRG-resident-macrophage-in-pain citation; (iv) one CIPN DRG neuropeptide/ATF3 citation. Specific wording for the Zhai acknowledgement (insert after §3.3 line 115): "This ISG-centric reading revisits the conclusion of the source study (ref [10]), which emphasized ion-channel and cytokine changes; the present reanalysis, using exhaustive permutation and compositional controls, elevates the interferon-stimulated-gene programme as the dominant axis at day 7."

### Item 6 — Human-translation limitation adequacy

**【Problem】** The human-translation limitation is stated (§5, line 181) but is under-played relative to the strength of the §4 clinical claim, and the species/timepoint extrapolation is not quantified.

**【Evidence】** §5 line 181: "nothing here is validated in humans, and the translation from a rat tibia tumour model to patients with bone metastases involves a species extrapolation that we have not bridged." This sentence exists and is honest, but it is a single clause at the end of a long limitations paragraph, while §4 (line 167) asserts a clinical-pharmacology "match" as a secure conclusion. The two are inconsistent in emphasis. Additionally, the day-7 rat timepoint is presented as "the" bone-cancer-pain state, but clinical bone-metastases pain is chronic and progressive; the paper never states whether day-7 rat corresponds to an early, mid, or late clinical stage. The B16F10 validation is mouse (different species) and CD45-depleted (different quantification), further distancing it from humans.

**【Why it matters】** If §4 keeps its confident clinical phrasing, §5 must be proportionally strengthened, or a clinician reviewer will see the paper as over-reaching clinically while paying only lip-service to the limitation. The cost of mis-translation is high: the markers (Cd68, Aif1, galanin, ATF3) are conserved, so *direction* may translate, but the specific IFN programme and its magnitude may not.

**【Specific fix】** Add to §5 after line 181 a concrete clause: "Accordingly, the mechanism-stratification implied in §4 is a hypothesis to be tested in human DRG (e.g., post-mortem or surgical samples from bone-metastases versus chemotherapy-exposed patients), not a validated clinical correlate; the day-7 rat timepoint in particular has no established human analogue and should not be read as a chronic bone-metastases state." And soften §4 per Item 4's fix so the two sections are consistent.

## § WHAT STANDS UP (verified correct; suspected but confirmed)

1. **Myeloid infiltration in bone-cancer-pain DRG is biologically plausible and the marker choice is valid.** Cd68/Aif1 are appropriate DRG myeloid markers; their large, exact-permutation-significant up-regulation in Walker-256 D7 vs paclitaxel (Cd68 Δ +3.42, Aif1 Δ +3.08, q=0.012, Table 3/§3.5) is internally consistent and concordant with the known innate-immune component of BCP (though the direct-support literature is spinal-cord microgliosis; see Item 5). The compositional-dilution explanation for the synchronous neuronal-module fall (§3.3 line 117; Table 1 pan-neuronal −5.20 sits among the neuronal targets) is a sound, well-argued inference, not a hand-wave.

2. **Galanin is correctly interpreted as injury-associated, and its direction is coherent.** Galanin rises in paclitaxel (+1.92) and in the B16F10 neuronal bin (+1.47, §3.8 line 330) — both nerve-injury contexts where galanin is a classic induced neuropeptide — and falls in bone cancer pain (−0.67). The cross-aetiology divergence (Δ −2.59, q=0.012) is consistent across §3.5 and Table 3. This is a textbook-correct use of an injury marker.

3. **The exact-permutation cross-aetiology statistics are appropriately conservative and the marker floor is honestly stated.** The p-floor mechanics (3/4,901 ≈ 6.1×10⁻⁴ for unpaired contrasts; 3/71 for shared-control; 2/71 for reversal, §2.7 lines 87–89; §5 line 179) are internally coherent and the leave-one-out sign-stability of the headline markers (Cd68 Δ ∈ [+3.28,+3.52], Aif1 ∈ [+2.88,+3.21], galanin ∈ [−2.69,−2.39]) is a strong robustness statement. As a domain reviewer I had suspected the q=0.012 might be a multiple-comparison artefact; the deposited leave-one-out ranges confirm it is not.

4. **The single-cell CD45-depletion caveat is honestly and repeatedly stated.** The paper does not hide that GSE292522 depleted CD45⁺ cells (§2.8 line 95; §3.8 lines 155, 157, 161), that myeloid cells number only ~46, and that the myeloid compartment is therefore untestable. This is exactly the kind of limitation a careful reviewer wants to see, and it is presented without defensiveness.

## § QUESTIONS FOR THE AUTHORS (answers needed; I will not guess)

1. **Day-7 vs day-14 transcriptional peak.** You report 0/1 DEGs at day 1, **4,600** at day 7, and only **235** at day 14 (§3.2 line 111). In the Walker-256 BCP literature, pain behaviours typically emerge at day 7 and *intensify* through days 14–21 (e.g., Miao 2010 Nav1.8 at days 16–19). A sharp day-7 peak that collapses by day 14 is unusual. Is day 7 an acute post-implantation innate-immune burst (tumour–stroma engagement) rather than the steady-state "bone cancer pain" programme? Did you test day 14 behaviours/genotypes, and does removing the contaminated sample GSM4506604 (which alone drops day-7 DEGs 4,600→3,041) preferentially attenuate the interferon module specifically?

2. **Type-I vs type-II interferon specificity.** Your CTRL interferon set (Irf1, Ifitm3, Ifi47, Bst2, Rsad2, Gbp2, Ubd, Ly6e, `_limma.py` lines 194–195) is classically type-I-induced, but Irf1 and several of these can also be driven by IFN-γ (type II) or TLR signalling. Did you run pathway/GSEA (e.g., GO "response to type I interferon" vs "type II") to justify the "type-I" label, or is it inferred from gene identity alone? This matters for the §3.3 headline.

3. **Gfap reconciliation.** Given your own companion data show the satellite-glia bin carries almost no Gfap (7 UMIs / 8 animals; §2.8 line 97), how do you interpret the bulk rat Gfap +1.33? Is it satellite-glia, Schwann-cell, or a bulk-composition artefact? Will you retain "glial activation (Gfap up)" as a secure conclusion (§4 line 167) or down-weight it (per Item 3)?

4. **Baruch [14] neuronal-compartment contradiction.** Your neuronal-bin test found Ifit2 *down* (q=0.046, §3.8 line 157), directly opposing Baruch's neuronal-intrinsic IFN claim. Why do you write "a conflict our design cannot adjudicate" rather than report this as negative evidence on the neuronal arm (reserving "cannot adjudicate" for the myeloid arm only)?

5. **Day-1 "null".** 0 (adjusted) / 1 (q<0.05) / 5 (q<0.10) DEGs at day 1 (§3.2). At n=4, is day-1 truly null or underpowered? Could a small early DRG response be masked by moderated-t shrinkage at this sample size?

6. **Human/clinical translation of markers.** Have you any human DRG evidence (even post-mortem or surgical) that Cd68/Aif1/galanin/ATF3 directions in bone-metastases vs chemotherapy-exposed patients match the rat findings? If not, will you reframe §4's clinical sentence as explicitly hypothesis-generating (per Item 4)?
