## Finding 1 — DESK-REJECT: the data/code repository cited in the availability statement does not exist

- **【Problem】** The manuscript's only reproducibility artefact is a GitHub URL that currently returns GitHub's "Page not found" 404 page.
- **【Evidence】** Data and code availability, `manuscript_final.md:183`: "Analysis code, artefacts and the test suite are deposited in the project repository (https://github.com/yyx-4113/cancer-pain-drg-virtual-perturbation), to be archived at Zenodo upon acceptance". I fetched that URL during this review: it is a **404**. The repository named in the manuscript is not publicly accessible. The only fallback offered is "to be archived at Zenodo upon acceptance" — a promise of future action, not present compliance.
- **【Why it matters】** For a paper whose entire empirical content is a computational reanalysis of public data, the code *is* the study. SCIE journals in this space (PLOS suite by policy; Springer Nature and most Elsevier titles via data-policy enforcement; Frontiers via their data policy) require that the availability statement point to something an editor or reviewer can access **at submission** — a live public repo, a "private-for-peer-review" repository link, or a reserved Zenodo DOI with restricted files. "Archived upon acceptance" is the single most common reason data-availability statements get bounced at triage, and a dead link combined with a promise is worse than either alone: it tells the editor the artefact does not exist anywhere yet. For a computational paper this is a desk-reject trigger, and it would also contradict ARRIVE 2.0 item 20 (data access) and the reproducibility claims made at `manuscript_final.md:101` ("A separate checker asserts every number reported below against the deposited CSV and JSON artefacts" — deposited where?).
- **【Specific fix】** Replace the last sentence of the availability statement with, e.g.: "All analysis code, result tables and the test suite are publicly available at https://github.com/yyx-4113/cancer-pain-drg-virtual-perturbation (archived at Zenodo, DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXX)." A Zenodo DOI can be reserved and the deposit placed in restricted mode before acceptance, then published on acceptance — this costs one hour and converts the promise into a citable identifier. If the author prefers to withhold the repo during review, deposit it now and add: "The repository has been submitted to Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXXX, restricted) and will be made public upon publication."

## Finding 2 — DESK-REJECT: five mandatory front-matter statements are absent

- **【Problem】** The manuscript has exactly one statement section (Data and code availability) and lacks Conflict of interests, Funding, Author contributions, Acknowledgements and an AI-use disclosure — all of which are mandatory at the large majority of SCIE venues.
- **【Evidence】** I searched the full text of `manuscript_final.md` for "Funding", "Conflict", "Competing", "Acknowledg", "Author contributions", "CRediT", "artificial intelligence" / "language model": no funding statement, no competing-interests declaration, no author-contributions/CRediT block, no acknowledgements, and no disclosure of AI assistance anywhere in the file. - **【Why it matters】** Competing-interests and funding statements are required by ICMJE-endorsed practice and by essentially every publisher's submission system; a submission system without them often cannot even complete the submission wizard. Author contributions (CRediT) are required by Springer, Elsevier, Wiley and most others — yes, even for single-author papers (the usual convention is a one-line statement covering all roles). The AI-use disclosure is now a hard requirement at COPE-member journals and most publishers since 2023–2025 (e.g., Elsevier, Springer Nature, Frontiers, PNAS all have explicit policies); given that this manuscript was prepared in an AI-assisted environment, an undeclared AI contribution, if later discovered, is a retraction-grade integrity issue, not a formatting one. Absent statements also interact badly with the single-author format (Finding 11): there is no colleague acknowledged, no funder, nothing that situates the work.

(or the actual grant number); (ii) "**Conflicts of interest:** The author declares no competing interests."; (iii) "**Author contributions:** Yongxin Yang: conceptualization, methodology, software, formal analysis, investigation, data curation, writing — original draft, writing — review & editing, visualization (sole author)."; (iv) an AI-use disclosure matching the venue's policy wording, e.g. "**Use of artificial intelligence:** AI-assisted tools were used for code development support and manuscript language editing under the author's direction, in accordance with the journal's AI-use policy; the author takes full responsibility for all content."; (v) optionally an Acknowledgements line.

## Finding 4 — Major: ARRIVE 2.0 Essential-10 coverage is incomplete and no checklist is supplied

- **【Problem】** This is animal research (secondary, but the venue will still treat it as animal data), and the manuscript omits or does not address at least five of the ARRIVE 2.0 Essential 10 items, without the completed checklist most venues now require.
- **【Evidence】** I checked the manuscript against the published ARRIVE 2.0 Essential 10 (Percie du Sert et al. 2020, *PLOS Biology*; confirmed against the author checklist). Present: item 1 study design (groups and experimental unit stated at `manuscript_final.md:43-45, 97`), item 7 statistical methods (§2.4, §2.6, §2.7 — unusually thorough), item 10 results with intervals (bootstrap CIs throughout). **Absent or unaddressed:** item 2b — no statement anywhere of how n = 4 per group was decided (a priori calculation? funding cap? precedent from refs [10,11]?); item 3 — no inclusion/exclusion criteria for animals or data points, and although the contaminated sample GSM4506604 is handled with exemplary sensitivity analysis (§3.1), the *criteria* (the 1% threshold, "established a priori or not") are never declared as ARRIVE demands; item 4 — randomisation is never mentioned (even "group allocation in the source studies was performed by the original investigators and is not described in their publications" would satisfy the item); item 5 — blinding, same; item 8 — **strain, sex, age and weight of the rats and mice are not reported anywhere in the manuscript**, and for pain biology sex is not a formality: pain processing is markedly sex-dimorphic and many journals (e.g., *PAIN*) enforce sex-as-a-biological-variable reporting specifically.
- **【Why it matters】** Editors at pain/neuroscience venues desk-check ARRIVE compliance almost mechanically because their reviewer pools demand it. A reanalysis cannot invent facts about the original experiments, but ARRIVE explicitly allows "reason for not reporting" per item — silence, however, reads as oversight, and the missing *sex* of the animals is the one item a specialist editor will notice instantly, because the paper's own conclusions (immune vs neuronal programmes) could in principle be sex-confounded across the two studies. The fix costs three sentences; omitting it risks a reviewer writing "the authors do not even tell us whether the animals were male", which derails the review.
- **【Specific fix】** Add a short "Animal subjects and ARRIVE reporting" paragraph at the end of §2.1, of this shape: "Animals: [strain, sex, age/weight as reported in refs 10, 11 and the corresponding GEO entries]. Group allocation and outcome assessment were performed by the original investigators; no randomisation or blinding information is available for the deposited data, and no animals were excluded by us post hoc. Our only analysis-stage exclusion criterion was the sarcomere-contamination threshold of 1% of total FPKM (§2.3), which was fixed before testing and flagged exactly one sample (GSM4506604), carried through leave-one-out sensitivity analysis (§3.2). Sample sizes were determined by the original studies; a retrospective power analysis for the key comparisons is reported in §3.6 and §3.8." Also fill the corresponding cells of a completed ARRIVE 2.0 checklist as a supplementary file, marking each non-applicable item with the reason. Additionally, extract and report the sex of the animals from refs [10,11] and [15] — if the GSE149648 and GSE160543 cohorts differ in sex composition, that must appear in Limitations, because it is an alternative explanation for the cross-aetiology divergence the paper claims.

## Finding 6 — Major: the per-compartment null is a genuine strengthening, but the manuscript twice overstates it as "confirming absence"

- **【Problem】** §3.8's compartment-resolved null is the best-controlled negative result in the paper and strengthens its posture, but two sentences convert "we detected no ISG up-regulation" into "absence is confirmed", which an underpowered n = 4 + 4 design cannot support.
- **【Evidence】** I recomputed the per-compartment ISG picture from `results/tables/sc_GSE292522_celltype_DE.csv`: across all eight compartments, **no interferon-stimulated gene is up-regulated at q < 0.05**; the only significant ISG changes are Ifit2 down in Neuron (logFC −0.049, q = 0.046) and Neuron.NF (−0.060, q = 0.041), Isg15 down in SatelliteGlia (−0.630, q = 0.007), Ifit1 down in SatelliteGlia (−0.523, q = 0.004) — all matching or exceeding the manuscript's claims. The whole-DRG numbers also reproduce: Sprr1a +1.554 (t = 54.49), Atf3 +0.835, Ifit2 q = 0.0417, Oas1c q = 0.0136, Gfap q = 0.0061, Cd68 and Aif1 flat (q = 0.78, 0.92), 1,517 up / 703 down, myeloid fraction 0.117% → 0.052% with MWU two-sided p = 0.114, Spearman vs Walker-256 day 7 = 0.098. So the underlying claim is correct. However: (i) the nearest positive blips are Neuron.NF Mx1 (t = +3.63, q = 0.054) and Ifit3 (q = 0.051) — inside one rounding step of significance, in a design whose own power analysis (T3, `negative_result_quantification.json`) concedes the myeloid-fraction comparison cannot detect d < 2.38; and (ii) Limitations (`manuscript_final.md:173`) says the extension "**confirms the absence** of an interferon programme in every DRG compartment", and §3.8 (`manuscript_final.md:153`) says "The interferon programme is therefore absent at the compartment level".
- **【Why it matters】** A reviewer who reads the power numbers two paragraphs earlier will object that the paper has just argued itself out of its own conclusion: if the design cannot detect d = 1.37 in the composition analysis, small ISG up-shifts could also be missed, and "confirmed absent" is then an over-claim that taints an otherwise exemplary negative result. The correct posture — "no ISG up-regulation was detected in any compartment, and the significant ISG changes were all down-regulations, in the same direction as the whole-DRG result" — is fully supported by the data and is actually more persuasive.
- **【Specific fix】** In §3.8, replace "The interferon programme is therefore absent at the compartment level" with "No ISG up-regulation was detected in any compartment at q < 0.05, and every significant ISG change was a down-regulation, in the same direction as the whole-DRG result; given the design's minimum detectable effect (§3.8 power analysis), we treat this as absence of detectable up-regulation rather than proven absence." In Limitations, replace "confirms the absence of an interferon programme in every DRG compartment" with "extends the whole-DRG null to every DRG compartment tested, showing the null is not an averaging artefact."

## Finding 7 — Major (strategic): the title and abstract frame foreground the contestable methodological claim; the durable biological finding should carry the paper

- **【Problem】** The headline unit of the paper — title suffix, running head, and the Conclusions' order of arguments — is "calibrated in-silico perturbation", a negative calibration result whose strongest honest form is design-scoped, while the robust, positive, generalisable finding (aetiology-dependent DRG programmes, interferon programme is model-specific) is structurally demoted.
- **【Evidence】** Title (`manuscript_final.md:1`): "Divergent dorsal root ganglion programs in bone cancer pain and chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy: **a calibrated in-silico perturbation analysis**" — the method tag is the claim-bearing phrase. Running head (`:3`): "Calibrated in-silico perturbation of cancer-pain DRG transcriptomes". The Conclusions (`:177`) open with the biology but spend 60% of their length on the perturbation verdict. Meanwhile the manuscript itself supplies the argument that the negative claim is design-scoped: §3.6 (`:139`) reports the reversal contrast is a 2-vs-2 comparison with minimum detectable Cohen's d = 5.65 — i.e. **the null result is partly a power artefact** — while the calibration failures that are design-independent (erythrocyte set ranked above every target, z = 7.39; network ρ = 0.991 with the additive baseline, which I reproduced as ρ = 0.992 from `etiology_reversal_point_estimates.csv`) are the genuinely portable findings. A title built on "cannot rank targets" invites the referee response "you have shown your n = 4 design cannot rank targets", which the paper concedes in Limitations.
- **【Why it matters】** Editors screen for the claim a paper will be remembered by and whether it can defend it. "In-silico perturbation cannot rank targets" is a universal negative that reviewers of benchmarking papers (cf. ref [9]) will test hard, and it is the weaker of the two claims as stated. "Bone cancer pain and CIPN drive divergent DRG programmes, and the interferon signature is model-specific" is positive, surprising, survives the paper's own exact tests (all q ≤ 0.027, recomputed), and has a clean clinical hook (mechanism-stratified analgesia). The perturbation calibration remains publishable as a *secondary* claim — a cautionary methods section with concrete rules (negative controls through the perturbation, degree-matched nulls, additive baseline, force-include targets) that is unusually actionable. Swapping the framing is honest: nothing needs to be softened, only re-ordered.
- **【Specific fix】** Retitle, e.g.: "Bone cancer pain and chemotherapy-induced neuropathy drive divergent dorsal root ganglion programs: myeloid/interferon versus neuronal-injury signatures, with a calibration of in-silico perturbation as a cautionary layer" (or shorter: "Divergent DRG programs in bone cancer pain and CIPN: an exact-test reanalysis with a calibrated perturbation control"). Running head: "Divergent cancer-pain DRG programs" (34 chars). Reorder Conclusions so the biology leads and the perturbation verdict appears as the second paragraph. Keep the methods-calibration rules in the Discussion exactly where they are — they are the paper's second citable asset.

## Finding 8 — Moderate: the universal negative "cannot rank targets" must be scoped to the design

- **【Problem】** Abstract and Conclusions state that in-silico perturbation "cannot rank" / "does not support" target ranking in the universal mood, but the paper's own power analysis shows the test was incapable of detecting effects smaller than d = 5.65, so the licensed conclusion is "this design provides no evidence for ranking".
- **【Evidence】** Abstract (`manuscript_final.md:23`): "The same data do not support module-level analgesic target ranking by in-silico perturbation" — this sentence is defensible (it names "the same data"). But Conclusions (`:177`) generalise: "in-silico perturbation results should carry the calibrations described here or be treated as hypothesis generation only", and §3.6 (`:139`) closes with "The perturbation screen is therefore hypothesis-generating, not a target-ranking tool". Verified numbers: T5 in `negative_result_quantification.json` — n_ko = 2, n_oe = 2, MDE Cohen's d = 5.65, detectable difference 0.425 reversal units, largest observed Δ = 0.274 (I recomputed min q = 0.165 and Δ = +0.274, CI 0.203–0.287 from `etiology_bootstrap_differences.csv`, per_contrast, Glial/immune knockout — exact match to the manuscript).
- **【Why it matters】** A null obtained from an underpowered test, stated unscoped, is the textbook statistical criticism and gives a hostile reviewer a one-line kill shot: "absence of evidence at d = 5.65 MDE is not evidence of absence." The calibration failures (erythrocyte z = 7.39; ρ = 0.991) *are* design-independent and carry the general point, but only if the power limitation is conceded where the general claim is made, not only in Limitations.
- **【Specific fix】** In the abstract Conclusions, replace "In-silico perturbation should be published with negative controls, degree-matched nulls and an additive baseline, or presented as hypothesis generation and nothing more" with: "At n = 4 per group the design is powered only for reversal-score differences exceeding ~0.42 units (d = 5.65), so it cannot license target ranking; whether better-powered designs change this conclusion is exactly what the negative controls and additive baseline reported here are needed to test." In Conclusions, insert after "underpowered to rank targets in any case (minimum detectable Cohen's d = 5.65 …)": "the design-independent calibration failures — a red-cell control outranking every analgesic target and a network model redundant with an additive baseline — are the general warning; the power ceiling is the local one."

## Finding 10 — Moderate: 16 references is thin for the claims made; at least six must-cites are missing

- **【Problem】** A paper whose two load-bearing arguments are (i) an exact-permutation methodology and (ii) a negative verdict on an entire class of perturbation methods cites 16 works, leaving its methodological lineage and its compositional-data logic uncited.
- **【Evidence】** The reference list (`manuscript_final.md:187-202`) contains 16 entries. Checked against the paper's own argument structure: the exact-permutation test is the paper's statistical signature ("exact p with no resampling error", `:19, :87`) yet there is **no citation for exact/permutation inference** (Ernst, M.D. 2004, *Statistical Science* 19:676–685, "Permutation methods: a basis for exact inference" is the standard reference). Network diffusion / random walk with restart — the core operator (§2.6, δ diffusion with closed form (1−α)(I−αS)⁻¹) — has **no methodological citation** (Cowen et al. 2017, *Nat Rev Genet* 18:600–614, "Network propagation: a universal amplifier of genetic associations", or the original Köhler et al. 2008 RWR paper). The compositional-interpretation argument — the paper's central biological caveat ("an influx of macrophages lowers the transcript share of every neuronal gene", `:35`; "the reversal score tracks cellular composition", `:23`) — cites **no compositional-data literature** (Gloor et al. 2017, *Front Microbiol* 8:2224; Robinson & Oshlack 2010, *Genome Biology* 11:R25). limma-trend is used but only Smyth 2004 is cited — **Law et al. 2014 / Ritchie et al. 2015 (*Nucleic Acids Res* 43:e47) is the canonical limma citation** for the trend/voom family. The perturbation-criticism frame cites scGen, GEARS and one 2025 benchmark, but not the compositional perturbation autoencoder (Lotfollahi et al. 2023, *Mol Syst Biol* 19:e11517) which is the method most often deployed in exactly this "rank targets from a response profile" mode. Finally, single-sex/small-n animal reporting practice (sex as a biological variable) is relevant to Finding 4 and uncited.
- **【Why it matters】** For a negative-methods claim in a computational venue, the reviewer will be someone from the perturbation-benchmarking or network-biology community; showing that the authors know the lineage (RWR, CPA, the benchmark literature) is what separates "independent replication of a known result" from "naive rediscovery". Missing statistical citations read as methodological insecurity in a paper that is otherwise conspicuously rigorous. Six to eight additional references is a one-hour fix that materially changes how the Methods section reads.
- **【Specific fix】** Add: Ernst 2004 (exact permutation); Cowen et al. 2017 (network propagation); Köhler et al. 2008 (RWR, optional); Gloor et al. 2017 and Robinson & Oshlack 2010 (compositional effects in RNA-seq); Ritchie et al. 2015 (limma) and/or Law et al. 2014 (trend/voom); Lotfollahi et al. 2023 (CPA). Cite Ernst at the §2.7 permutation passage, Cowen at §2.6 diffusion, Gloor/Robinson at the §1 compositional paragraph and at §3.3's composition interpretation, Ritchie/Law at §2.4, CPA beside refs [7,8].
