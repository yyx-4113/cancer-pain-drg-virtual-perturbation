To the Editorial Office of PLOS ONE

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript entitled "Divergent dorsal root ganglion programs in bone cancer pain and chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy: an exact cross-study reanalysis and a negative-control calibration of in-silico perturbation" for publication as a Research Article in PLOS ONE.

This work is a methodological reanalysis of two same-platform public rat dorsal root ganglion (DRG) transcriptome studies (GSE149648, GSE160543) together with an independent mouse single-cell dataset (GSE292522). We keep two questions apart. First, do the DRG programmes of bone cancer pain and chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy diverge, and along which axes? Second, once the in-silico perturbation layer is calibrated against negative controls, degree-matched nulls, an oracle anchor and an additive baseline, does it add anything to the first answer? We report an exact, permutation-based divergence between the two aetiologies (myeloid and interferon-associated in bone cancer pain; neuronal-injury-driven in the paclitaxel/oxaliplatin neuropathy models), and a negative, internally calibrated result for the perturbation layer: it correlates 0.991 with an additive model, ranks an erythrocyte control above every analgesic target, and is anti-selective (negative-control modules carry larger standardised effects than targets; Mann-Whitney U p = 0.0019).

We submit to PLOS ONE because the journal evaluates scientific soundness rather than perceived novelty or significance, and this manuscript is a good fit on three grounds. First, it is a fully reproducible computational reanalysis: analysis code, deposited CSV/JSON artefacts, and a test suite that re-derives every reported number are released under a single tagged version with pinned software dependencies. Second, it makes an honest negative-result contribution, demonstrating with calibrated controls why in-silico target ranking fails in small-n bulk tissue and what minimum reporting is required to see that failure. Third, it addresses a method (in-silico perturbation / virtual screening) that is widely used in pain and analgesia research yet rarely benchmarked against its own negative controls.
